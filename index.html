zine_machine

A zine press made for creative in-the-field storytelling. Fill your panels with images and words — with optional riso inks, halftones, the works — then print on letter-sized paper. Choose the one-sheet mini (eight pages, one cut, no staples) or a stapled booklet (up to 24 half-letter pages, printed double-sided). The press sheets handle all the page-shuffling math for you.

  1. Print the PDF single-sided on letter paper, landscape, at 100% scale (no “fit to page” shrinking).
  2. Fold in half the long way (top edge to bottom edge). Crease hard, then unfold.
  3. Fold in half the short way (left edge to right edge) and keep it folded.
  4. Cut along the pink ✂ line — from the folded edge to the crease, through the middle of the sheet. Unfold.
  5. Refold the long way, then push both ends toward the middle. The slit pops open into a diamond — keep pushing until the panels collapse into a stack, and flatten it with your cover in front.

Yes, half the pages print upside down. That’s the trick — they turn right-side up when folded.

  1. Print double-sided on letter paper, landscape, at 100% scale. Set duplex to flip on short edge — this is the step everyone gets wrong, and it’s the difference between a zine and a puzzle.
  2. Keep the sheets in printed order. Sheet 1 holds your covers and goes on the outside; the last sheet nests innermost.
  3. Stack the sheets with every front side facing the same way, then fold the whole stack in half along the dashed center line.
  4. Staple twice along the fold — a long-arm stapler is ideal. No long-arm? Open the stack flat, put a folded towel underneath, and press a regular stapler down through the crease.
  5. Crease hard with the back of a spoon and trim the open edge with a ruler and blade if you want it flush.

Each page prints next to its faraway partner — page 1 sits beside the last page. The fold sorts them out.
